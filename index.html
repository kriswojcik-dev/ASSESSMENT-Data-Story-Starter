DATA STORY / JAPAN / 1960–2024

Japan's Long Life

How Japan gained 16 years of life expectancy over six decades.

In 1960, life expectancy at birth in Japan was approximately 68 years. By 2024, it had reached 84 years, an increase of 16 years. This improvement represents a major shift in the country's mortality patterns over six decades. However, the pace of improvement has not remained constant. Japan experienced particularly large gains during the earlier decades, while more recent improvements have been smaller. Data from the World Bank illustrates both the remarkable increase in longevity and the slowing rate of progress. Although life expectancy describes population-level mortality conditions rather than predicting an individual's lifespan, it provides a useful measure of how Japan's health outcomes have changed.

01 / THE STORY

A Nation Living Longer

Japan's life expectancy at birth increased by 16 years between 1960 and 2024, but the rate of improvement has slowed compared with the earlier decades.

Life expectancy at birth estimates the number of years a newborn would live if the mortality patterns of a particular year remained unchanged. It does not predict the actual lifespan of an individual person.

Source and method: World Bank, World Development Indicators, Life expectancy at birth, total (years) — Japan . Annual observations were selected and rounded to the nearest whole number. Differences were calculated using those displayed rounded values.

Data Transformations

Values were selected from the annual dataset for 1960, 1970, 1980, 1990, 2000, 2010, 2020, and 2024. Values were rounded to the nearest whole number. Differences and cumulative changes were calculated from the rounded observations. The editorial graphics visualize selected comparisons rather than reproducing every annual value.
